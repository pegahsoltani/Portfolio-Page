CPP A/B Testing

From manual experimentation to more reliable creative optimization decisions

MobileAction | Apple Ads Campaign Management Platform | Product Manager | Approx. six months from April 2025

Project Overview

CPP A/B Testing is a feature within MobileAction’s Apple Ads Campaign Management Platform that helps advertisers compare Custom Product Pages (CPPs) through automated experiments and statistically supported results. The initiative progressed from product definition and customer validation to a limited rollout and broader public availability as part of the enterprise offering. When I joined the initiative in April 2025, customer demand for CPP testing had already been established. The remaining challenge was to understand how advertisers conducted experiments, why their existing comparisons could be unreliable, and what a usable solution needed to accomplish. As Product Manager, I led further customer discovery, evaluated existing workflows, prepared product requirements and initial wireframes, worked across Data Science, Design, Engineering and QA, and coordinated product validation and release preparation.

Product Context

MobileAction provides tools for mobile app marketers to manage, analyze and optimize advertising performance. In Apple Ads, advertisers can use alternative App Store Custom Product Pages with different screenshots and creative messages. For example, a gaming app might emphasize competitive gameplay for one audience and social features for another. User-acquisition teams and agencies operating across campaigns, audiences and geographic markets need to decide which creative is most effective for a given context. Choosing the right CPP can influence the journey from a tap on an advertisement to an app install. But identifying the stronger variant requires a meaningful comparison, not just choosing the largest observed number.

Problem Identification & Customer Research

The market need for CPP testing predated my involvement. Customer Success and Sales had heard advertisers describe manual testing processes that required recurring setup, monitoring and interpretation. I worked with Customer Success to identify relevant accounts and spoke directly with advertisers about their actual decision-making workflows. Instead of beginning with a list of requested features, I asked what decision they wanted to make and how they were making it today.

What the research revealed

Parallel testing: Some advertisers created similar campaigns simultaneously, assigning a different CPP to each. Because Apple Ads controlled traffic allocation, variants could receive unequal exposure. Differences in observed performance were therefore not necessarily attributable to the creative alone. Switch testing: Other advertisers used one creative for a period before switching to another. This introduced questions about test duration and comparability across hours, days and seasonal conditions. Reverse testing: Some advertisers wanted to discover which audience or keyword/ad-group configuration worked best with a particular CPP, rather than selecting a CPP for an established audience. Across these approaches, three questions kept recurring: How could we reduce the effect of external variables? How long should experiments run? And when could a result be considered reliable enough to inform a campaign decision?

Reframing the Problem

The opportunity was initially framed around eliminating manual effort. Discovery showed that automation alone would not solve the deeper challenge: advertisers were making optimization decisions using comparisons affected by traffic distribution, timing, seasonality and potentially insufficient sample sizes. The product opportunity was not simply to make testing easier. It was to make the resulting creative decisions more reliable.

Product Opportunity & Hypothesis

For advertisers, an integrated experimentation capability could reduce repetitive campaign operations and provide a more structured basis for evaluating CPP performance. For MobileAction, it also offered potential differentiation: at the time of our research, we had not identified an equivalent capability among the competing Apple Ads management tools we reviewed. We considered selling CPP A/B Testing separately versus including it in the existing enterprise package. We chose enterprise packaging because the capability complemented other advanced campaign-management and optimization workflows. Hypothesis: If we automated experiment execution, reduced the influence of external factors and supported statistical validation, advertisers could compare CPPs with less manual work and greater confidence, potentially improving subsequent creative decisions.

Evaluating the Product Approach

Customer Success helped verify whether the testing approaches we heard in interviews also appeared across other customer accounts. I brought the workflow problems and product questions to Data Science, particularly around traffic variability, sample sizes, switching periods, test duration and confidence interpretation. Data Science owned the methodology; I owned translating customer needs into product questions and understandable requirements. With Engineering, I assessed the feasibility of duplicating testing entities, activating and deactivating them, collecting performance data and handling Apple Ads integration constraints. I created initial wireframes and worked with Product Design on an understandable flow for setup, monitoring and results interpretation.

Defining the MVP

The initial product scope included all four configurations, grouped under Parallel and Switch methods: single-ad-group Parallel (multiple CPPs for one ad group), multiple-ad-group Parallel (multiple ad groups using one CPP), Ad Switch (rotating ads inside one ad group), and Ad Group Switch (rotating duplicated ad groups). This reflected the different testing objectives uncovered during discovery. The end-to-end journey was designed to be straightforward: select the campaign or ad group and variants; choose the relevant method; review statistical precision and calculated duration; launch the automated experiment; and track performance, comparison benchmarks and confidence signals. Automated entity setup and experiment execution reduced tasks that advertisers previously managed manually. The dedicated testing table focused on predefined metrics and confidence information. Custom metric selection inside that table and advanced automated insight generation were outside the original MVP.

Key Product Trade-offs

Trade-off A: Duration flexibility versus statistical reliability

Advertisers wanted to control test duration, particularly for seasonal or time-sensitive campaigns. But the necessary runtime depended on traffic, sample size and the degree of precision required. An unrestricted manual override could encourage users to stop before enough evidence existed. We prioritized statistically calculated duration over unrestricted user-defined durations in the initial release, keeping additional flexibility as a possible later enhancement.

Trade-off B: Reporting breadth versus platform reuse

Some users needed more metrics than the CPP results table displayed. Rather than duplicate detailed reporting functionality, we kept the experiment view focused. Users could filter the test-generated campaigns and entities in the main Ads Manager dashboard to examine additional performance metrics. This supported deeper analysis without expanding the MVP into another full reporting tool.

Design, Technical Constraints & Delivery

I prepared a PRD covering the purpose, four testing configurations, user flows, functional requirements, errors, edge cases, acceptance criteria and supporting designs. I coordinated planning with Frontend, Backend and QA, helped prioritize the backlog using engineering estimates, and clarified behavior during development.

Delayed performance updates

Apple Ads performance data updated overnight. Newly created tests could initially show no results even when setup had succeeded. We introduced a scheduled-for-midnight state and delayed meaningful result display until data became available, reducing confusion over apparently empty results.

Campaign-status latency

Activating and deactivating test entities through the Apple Ads API was not instantaneous. The team accounted for status propagation delays through buffer periods between relevant operations to reduce transient mismatches and protect experiment execution.

Acceptance validation

I personally checked user flows against requirements and reviewed QA findings while QA owned formal testing. During implementation and limited rollout, issues affecting statistical correctness, test execution, or completion took priority over secondary enhancements.

Beta Validation & Product Iteration

We treated limited customer testing as another learning phase. I gathered feedback both directly from customers and through Customer Success, maintained a shared feedback space, and prioritized problems affecting real-world experiment usability. Low-traffic campaigns: Some generated impractically long estimated durations. With Data Science, we introduced traffic eligibility requirements so customers could not initiate tests unlikely to reach useful results in a reasonable timeframe.

Confidence comprehension: Customers sometimes struggled to interpret statistical confidence. We improved supporting explanations and Help Center education. Experiment control: Customers sometimes needed to abandon or replace an active test. We introduced a Stop action so the same campaign could later be used in another experiment. Early confidence volatility: Confidence figures could fluctuate sharply before enough data had accumulated. We delayed confidence display until the result was sufficiently stable to communicate responsibly.

Public Release & Measurement

After addressing significant issues raised during limited usage, we expanded availability as part of MobileAction’s enterprise offering. I worked with Customer Success and Marketing on feature readiness and product education and prepared public Help Center documentation covering configuration, testing methods, duration, results monitoring and interpretation. Success was not defined solely as the number of tests created. I looked at experiment creation and completion, adoption among relevant accounts, whether customers applied the findings, and support issues affecting the core workflow. These signals helped distinguish initial interest from customers actually using the feature to make decisions.

Outcomes & Impact

Multiple enterprise customers completed experiments and applied their findings to campaign decisions. Results varied: not every experiment produced a clear winner, reinforcing that the purpose of testing was better evidence rather than guaranteed uplift. CPP A/B Testing moved from established market demand to public availability and strengthened MobileAction’s differentiated enterprise offering. The capability drew interest during enterprise proof-of-concept conversations and contributed to an enterprise customer win. These are qualitative outcomes; customer names, specific campaign performance figures, and deal amounts are intentionally excluded.

Reflection

This project reinforced that a statistically defensible feature is not automatically a practical or understandable feature. Discovery exposed the limitations of manual comparisons; collaboration with Data Science brought reliability into the product definition; and beta testing demonstrated the importance of realistic traffic requirements, clear confidence communication and appropriate user control. The experience strengthened my approach to balancing statistical reliability, technical feasibility and customer usability. It also reminded me that release is only one point in the product lifecycle: the real test is whether customers can interpret results and confidently apply what they learn.
